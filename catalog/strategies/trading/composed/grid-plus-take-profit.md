# Grid plus take-profit

Small repeated trades, plus a plan for the big move if it comes.

A [grid](../basic/grid.md) sells its inventory back into the range. That's the design — but it means a genuine breakout finds you with nothing left to sell into it. Setting aside separate inventory for higher targets fixes that.

## How it works

You hold 4,000 ALGO.

- **2,500 ALGO** becomes grid inventory, trading between $0.14 and $0.22
- **1,500 ALGO** is committed to a [take-profit ladder](../basic/take-profit.md) at $0.30, $0.40 and $0.50

Day to day the grid does the work. If ALGO breaks out and runs to $0.42, the grid sold its inventory on the way up around $0.22 — but the ladder is still there, selling into real strength.

The grid cannot touch ladder inventory. That's what makes it still be there when it matters.

## Adapting it

Change the split by conviction. More in the ladder if you expect a breakout; more in the grid if you expect chop.

Refill the ladder from grid profits over time, so the long-term exit grows out of short-term trading.

Set the lowest ladder rung clearly above the top of the grid range, or the two are competing for the same move.

## What to watch for

**Ladder inventory does nothing in a flat market.** It sits, unsold and untradeable, for as long as the breakout takes. Possibly forever.

**The grid still suffers in a downtrend**, and holding back inventory doesn't change that.

**Overlapping ranges defeat the purpose.** If the ladder's bottom rung sits inside the grid's range, you've just built a more complicated grid.
