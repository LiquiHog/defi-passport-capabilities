# Royalty revenue

Income that arrives unpredictably, turned into something you can plan around.

Secondary royalties are real money and terrible at funding fixed costs. A good month covers everything; three quiet months cover nothing. Treating royalties as a separate stream — rather than as budget — is what makes them useful.

## The approach

Royalties arrive irregularly. Each time they do, allocate in a fixed order:

1. **Top up the runway.** Bring operating funding back to target first, always.
2. **Fund the buffer.** Cover known irregular costs.
3. **Split the rest.** Team, treasury, community — whatever you agreed.

The order is the whole discipline. Projects that split royalties first and top up with what's left are solvent in good months and stressed in quiet ones.

## A worked month

18,000 USDC of royalties, against a 6,000 monthly burn and a runway target of twelve months:

| Step | Amount | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Runway top-up | 12,000 | Back to twelve months |
| Buffer | 2,000 | Known costs covered |
| Split three ways | 4,000 | Team, treasury, community fund |

In a quiet month all of it goes to runway and nobody gets a split. That's the system working — the project doesn't stop.

## Adapting it

Automate the split with [revenue splitting](../revenue-splitting.md) once the runway is topped up.

Lend the runway so it earns between top-ups. See [idle capital](../../../strategies/lending/idle-capital.md).

Set a threshold — no split below a monthly royalty figure — so quiet months automatically protect the project.

## What to watch for

**Royalties decline.** Trading volume falls as a collection ages. A budget built on this year's royalties is usually optimistic about next year's.

**They can stop entirely.** Marketplaces have changed royalty enforcement before and may again. Never build fixed costs on royalty income alone.

**Good months are not the new normal.** One strong month is a top-up, not a raise. This is the most common way projects overextend.

**Agree gross or net in advance.** Whether the split applies before or after costs is the dispute that ends collaborations.
