# Creator revenue

Uneven income, steady obligations, and a way to stop the gap being stressful.

A creator earning from mints, royalties, commissions and sponsorships has months that are excellent and months that aren't. Rent is unmoved by this. The problem isn't the income — it's that nothing smooths it.

## The approach

Treat every payment that arrives as three decisions, in a fixed order:

1. **Top up the buffer.** Back to your target — three to six months of costs.
2. **Pay yourself a fixed amount.** The same number every month, regardless of what came in.
3. **Anything left** goes to long-term savings.

Paying yourself a fixed salary out of a buffer is the entire trick. Good months build the buffer, quiet months draw on it, and your personal finances stop tracking your worst week.

## A worked setup

A creator averaging 8,000 USDC a month, with 4,000 in living costs:

| Purpose | Amount | State |
|---|---|---|
| Buffer — 6 months | 24,000 | **Locked**, drawn monthly |
| Monthly salary | 4,000 | [Recurring payment](../commerce/recurring-payments.md) to a personal wallet |
| Long-term savings | Surplus | [Rebalancing](../../strategies/portfolio/rebalancing.md) or [lending](../../strategies/lending/idle-capital.md) |

A 15,000 month tops up the buffer and adds to savings. A 1,500 month pays the salary anyway.

## Adapting it

Raise the salary deliberately, once, when the buffer has been consistently full for a couple of quarters. Not in a good month.

Convert volatile income to something stable on receipt, so the buffer holds value rather than exposure.

Split income automatically with collaborators. See [revenue splitting](../commerce/revenue-splitting.md).

## What to watch for

**Set the salary from your worst year, not your best.** A salary sized to a good run is a salary you'll have to cut.

**Tax is not surplus.** If you owe it, it isn't yours. Hold it in something stable from the moment you know.

**A good month is not a raise.** This is the most common way freelance income gets away from people.

**Don't invest the buffer in anything volatile.** The buffer's job is being there. Lending is reasonable; a trading strategy isn't.
