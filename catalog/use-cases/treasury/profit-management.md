# Profit allocation

Decide where gains go before you have them, so you never have to decide in the moment.

Profit that arrives with no plan sits wherever it landed, which usually means it keeps trading in the strategy that made it. Sometimes that's right. Often you'd rather it funded operations, grew the reserve, or paid for the automation that earned it.

## The decision

Three destinations, chosen per strategy:

| Destination | Use it when |
|---|---|
| **Your wallet** | You want gains out of active management |
| **Another strategy** | You want compounding — trading gains growing a portfolio |
| **Running costs** | You want automation paying for itself |

You set either a percentage of each gain or a fixed amount, so you can take a steady slice or skim only above a threshold.

## A worked split

A protocol running three strategies:

| Strategy | Profit goes to | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Grid | 50% to the portfolio, 50% stays | Compound, while the grid grows too |
| Rebalancing | Stays | Long-term holdings should keep growing |
| Lending | Running costs | Steady yield covers ongoing automation |

Nothing needs a decision after that. Each strategy's gains have somewhere to be.

## Adapting it

Point everything at operations while a treasury is young and needs runway, then switch to compounding once it's comfortable.

Send a fixed amount rather than a percentage when you want predictable income rather than a variable cut.

Review it quarterly. A treasury that needed income last year may want compounding now.

## What to watch for

**Assets have to match.** Profits top up a strategy holding the same asset. Nothing is converted for you.

**Routing adds funding, not action.** The destination strategy runs on its own conditions, now with more behind it.

**Sending everything away means the source never grows.** A strategy that keeps nothing stays exactly the same size forever.

**Not everything received is profit.** Sale proceeds include your original capital. Keep principal and gains separate in your accounting or you'll route away the thing that was generating returns.
