# Writing a strategy page

A strategy page answers one question: what does this do for me, and when would I not want it?

**Open with the outcome.** One line on what the reader gets. Not how it works — what it does for them.

**Then the situation.** Why would someone want this? What problem does it solve that the obvious alternative doesn't?

**Show it with real numbers.** Actual assets and actual amounts. "1,000 USDC buying ALGO at $0.15" beats "a purchase budget with an output condition" every time.

**Give a variation or two.** How would someone adapt this to a slightly different goal?

**End with what to watch for.** The honest failure modes, in their own section. Not scattered through the page where they undercut the explanation — collected at the end where someone deciding can weigh them.

## Voice

Write for someone who understands money but not this system. Say "buy low and sell high repeatedly" before you say "grid". Expand jargon on first use or replace it.

Avoid hedges — "supported", "eligible", "compatible" — which make capabilities sound provisional. If something works, say it works. If it has a limit, name the limit.

Be concrete about risk. "A sustained trend leaves you holding a falling asset" is useful. "Market conditions may affect performance" is not.
