# Writing a use case page

A use case starts with a person or project in a situation, not with a feature.

**Open with the situation.** Who is this, and what's the problem? A reader should recognize themselves in the first two sentences or move on.

**Explain why it's hard.** What makes this genuinely awkward without a passport? If the answer is "nothing much", the page isn't needed.

**Show the setup.** A table of allocations with real amounts, and what state each part is in — locked, committed, available.

**Follow the money.** Where does income arrive, what order do things get funded in, what happens when it runs out?

**End with what to watch for.** The mistakes people actually make in this situation, stated plainly.

## Voice

Write for the person in the situation, not for someone evaluating software. A DAO treasurer cares about contributors getting paid, not about payment rule semantics.

Use real amounts throughout. Someone should be able to swap in their own numbers without translating.

Be honest about the hard parts. A use case that makes something sound effortless is less trustworthy than one that names the two decisions you'll have to get right.
